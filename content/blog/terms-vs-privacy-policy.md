<p>When setting up a website, you'll hear that you need both "Terms" and "Privacy" pages. Are they the same thing? Definitely not — they cover different risks, point in different directions, and are required by different laws.</p>

<h3>Privacy Policy: Your Data Practices</h3>
<p>A Privacy Policy is about your <strong>users' data</strong>. It explains:</p>
<ul>
  <li>What data you collect (emails, IP addresses, names, device IDs, payment info).</li>
  <li>How you use it (account management, marketing, analytics, AI processing).</li>
  <li>Who you share it with (service providers, ad partners, affiliates).</li>
  <li>What rights users have (access, deletion, opt-out of sale/sharing, opt-out of profiling).</li>
  <li>How users can exercise those rights and how long their data is kept.</li>
</ul>
<p>This is the document that satisfies privacy regulators. As of 2026, that includes the EU/UK GDPR, the federal HIPAA and COPPA regimes in the U.S., and a fast-growing patchwork of state consumer privacy laws — California (CCPA/CPRA), Virginia, Colorado, Connecticut, Utah, Texas, Oregon, Montana, Iowa, Tennessee, Delaware, New Jersey, New Hampshire, Maryland, Indiana, Minnesota, Rhode Island, Kentucky, Nebraska, and counting — 20 states now have comprehensive privacy laws in effect, with more on the way. If your site has any users in those jurisdictions, you need a Privacy Policy and a way to honor data-subject requests.</p>

<h3>Terms & Conditions: Defining the Rules</h3>
<p>Terms & Conditions (also called Terms of Service or Terms of Use) are about <strong>your website's rules</strong> — the contract between you and the people using it. A solid set typically covers:</p>
<ul>
  <li>What users can and cannot do on your site (acceptable use).</li>
  <li>Intellectual property (your content stays yours; user-generated content licensing).</li>
  <li>Disclaimers of warranty and limitations of liability.</li>
  <li>Account termination rights (you can ban bad actors).</li>
  <li>Governing law, venue, and dispute resolution (arbitration, class-action waivers where allowed).</li>
  <li>Subscription, refund, and auto-renewal terms — increasingly required by U.S. state "click-to-cancel" laws. Note: the FTC's 2024 amended Negative Option Rule was vacated by a federal appellate court in July 2025; the FTC is pursuing new rulemaking as of early 2026.</li>
</ul>
<p>Unlike a Privacy Policy, T&amp;Cs aren't always strictly required by statute — but without them you have almost no protection if a user misuses your service, infringes your IP, or sues you over an outage.</p>

<h3>Do You Need Both?</h3>
<p>Yes. The Privacy Policy keeps you legally compliant with data-protection laws. Terms & Conditions protect your business, your IP, and your liability exposure. Together they cover both halves of the legal risk: the data going in, and the activity happening on the way out.</p>
